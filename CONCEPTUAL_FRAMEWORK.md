# Conceptual Notes: *a (gesture toward) linguistic rewilding*

## Gesture Over Mastery

The title—*a (gesture toward) linguistic rewilding*—frames the project as a directional motion rather than an accomplished translation or a claim of mastery. A gesture is partial and provisional: it points toward nonhuman vocal space without pretending that an algorithm built from human orthography, Latin-alphabet tokenization, and typographic rules escapes human mediation.

Within the pipeline, "rewilding" operates not as decay or mutation away from a human standard, but as a process of translation—at times from human prose into nonhuman vocalizations, and at times from one nonhuman vocalization into another as overlapping waves pass across the same coordinates.

---

## Operational Limits, Judgment, and Reading

Outside structured creative or academic constraints, it is easy to treat reading "in the wild" as an exercise without boundaries—as if stepping back from control leaves a text simply open and a reader wholly free to read however they please. Working within explicit operational limits—whether an academic creative-writing context, a NaNoGenMo threshold, or `book.py`'s character map and dynamic averages—shifts attention to what boundaries actually do.

Constraints narrow the field of what is produced and how it can be encountered, yet they do not settle judgment. An apparatus can define thresholds, calculate spans, and stage visual cues on the page, while the act of judgment remains with the reader—who can still refuse the gesture, read it against its grain, or treat it as an opening, a gimmick, or a dead end.

---

## Method, Oversimplification, and Typography

The use of two typefaces (`baskerville.ttf` for unaltered source text and `gwendolyn.ttf` for rewilded spans) functions as a procedural simplification rather than a claim about how nonhuman voices are structured. At any finer level of acoustic or linguistic specificity, distinct nonhuman vocalizations would require distinct representations rather than being grouped under a single alternate script. The typographic binary makes the boundary of each translation event legible on the page while leaving that simplification visible.

---

## *Zoonomia* and *Nothing About Us Without Us*

Using Erasmus Darwin's *Zoonomia; or, the Laws of Organic Life* as the source corpus introduces a wry inflection of the principle *"nothing about us without us"*: a treatise written to catalog and explain organic life beyond the purely human is progressively overtaken and re-articulated by nonhuman vocalizations.

---

## Holding Contradictory Positions Open

Rather than resolving its tensions into a single thesis, the text operates as a gymnasium or playground—a site to test friction rather than a manual or instructor. Any move within such a system can register differently depending on vantage point: what looks like an advance or opening from one position may read as a retreat or a surrender of legibility and structure from another. Recognizing what a work makes possible does not require treating its categories as fixed or permanent.
